## 1. Foundations
- **An ad works when three things line up:** a clear problem, a relevant offer, and the right person at the right time. Miss one and you waste money. Miss two and you waste a lot.
- **There are three levels:**
  - **Campaign:** why you're running it (the objective).
  - **Ad set:** who sees it, where, and for how much. Most money is made or lost here.
  - **Ad:** what people see (creative, primary text, headline, CTA button).
- **Pick the objective that matches your actual goal.** The algorithm optimizes for exactly what you choose. A Traffic campaign gets you people who click a lot but rarely buy.
  - Want DMs → Messages (the default for local businesses: highest intent and fastest feedback)
  - Want names and emails → Leads
  - Want website sales → Sales/Conversions
  - Want views → Video views (only with a clear reason)
- **A good offer** is a specific fix for a painful problem, with a timeline and a clear next step. Weak: "Expert HVAC services, call today." Strong: "AC out? We'll diagnose in 60 min and fix same day. Message us to book." The tighter the offer, the better the ad performs.
- **Never boost a post.** Always use Ads Manager.
- **Answer these five questions before launching:** Who is it for? What's their problem right now? What's your solution? What proof do you have? What one action do you want them to take?
- **Priority mantra:** goal > audience > creative.

## 2. Launch setup (the default recipe)
- **Campaign:** Messages objective. Name it as service–objective–date. Leave special ad categories, A/B test and Advantage campaign budget off.
- **Ad set:**
  - Conversion location: messaging apps
  - Budget: $10–20/day ($5 works, but learning takes longer)
  - No end date
  - Location: your city plus a 10–15 mile radius. Age: 28–65+. Gender: all.
  - Targeting: broad, at most 1–2 interests. Narrow targeting usually hurts.
  - Placements: manual. Use Facebook feed, Instagram feed and Instagram stories. Marketplace is optional for local businesses. Skip Audience Network, in-stream, Reels and the Messenger inbox, because they bring cheap clicks that don't convert.
- **Ad:** a single image or video (no carousels yet). Real phone photos beat stock photos, and before/after shots work best. CTA is "Send message" for services, "Learn more" for landing pages.
- **After it goes live, don't touch it for 72 hours** (the learning phase) unless something is broken.
- **What to expect for local services:** about $2–6 per message, CTR of 1% or more, first lead within 1–2 days. A tree-trimming ad got leads for under $5, versus $50+ on Thumbtack.

## 3. Copywriting
- **Write for the 3 S's:** Scroll (does the hook stop people?), Skim (is the offer clear at a glance?), Spark (does it make them act?).
- **Use the formula Hook → Problem → Offer → CTA.**
  - **Hook:** put the most effort into the first line. Lead with the pain or with curiosity, never "We're a family-owned business."
  - **Problem:** at most two sentences of empathy, no pitch yet.
  - **Offer:** be specific and use numbers ("same day", "90-minute job", "free quote"), not vague words like "fast."
  - **CTA:** tell people exactly what to do ("Message us for a quote in 30 seconds"). Avoid "Contact us if interested."
- **Length:** use short copy when the photo sells, and longer copy when the service needs explaining or is expensive.
- **Headlines** should lead with the problem ("AC not cooling?"), the result ("Fixed in 90 min"), or the offer ("$25 off first job"). Don't write taglines.
- **Ranking of creative that works:** before/after photos, then action shots of your team, then faces or selfie-style shots, then the product in use. Use text overlays only if you test them.
- **Tone:** write like a person, at a 5th–7th grade reading level, in short sentences. ("Been in business 15 years. Still show up on time.")

## 4. Testing and optimization
**Key numbers to watch (benchmarks are industry-dependent):**

| Metric | Good | Problem signal |
|---|---|---|
| CTR | ≥1% | <0.5% → fix the hook or image |
| CPC / cost per message | <$2 (<$1 great) | >$3 → creative or targeting is off |
| CPM | $10–25 (local) | $40+ → audience too narrow |
| Link clicks vs. landing page views | roughly equal | far more clicks → site is slow or broken |
| ROAS | 2–4x+ | 1x = breakeven, which really means a loss after overhead |

- **A/B test one variable at a time,** in this order: creative → primary text → headline → audience → placement → objective. Changing the objective resets learning.
- **How to run a test:** use Meta's Experiments tool, or duplicate the ad set, change one thing, split the budget evenly, and run it 3–5 days. Keep the winner and kill the loser.
- **Diagnosis:**
  - No clicks → fix the image, then the hook, then the offer, then shorten the copy.
  - Clicks but no conversions → the landing page or CTA is the problem.
  - Messages but no sales → you're replying too slowly, sounding robotic, or priced too high.
  - Expensive clicks → widen targeting or swap the image. Ugly real photos often beat polished Canva graphics.
- **Retargeting:** once you have a few hundred interactions, run a $3–5/day ad to people who engaged but didn't convert (e.g. "Saw you checking us out…").
- **Ad fatigue:** when CTR drops or CPC rises, refresh the creative every 2–3 weeks. Change one element at a time.

## 5. Scaling and automation
- **Only scale when all of these are true:** CTR is above 1%, cost per lead is profitable, results have been consistent for 5–7 days, and you're getting real customers rather than just engagement.
- **Two ways to scale:**
  - **Slow:** raise the budget 20–30% every 3–4 days. If performance drops, go back to the last stable budget.
  - **Duplicate:** copy the winning ad set at 2–3x the budget and leave the original running as a safety net.
- **Don't scale creative older than about 3 weeks.** Refresh it first.
- **Scaling framework by daily spend:**
  - $5–20: test and gather data for 7 days.
  - $20–200: optimize creative and targeting, and start scaling slowly.
  - $100–250: duplicate winners and add new audiences.
  - $250–500: add retargeting and new offers, and rotate creative weekly.
- **Funnel structure:**
  - Cold layer: $20–100/day, broad or lookalike audience.
  - Retargeting layer: $3–10/day, website visitors and engagers, with urgency copy.
  - Optional nurture layer: testimonials and educational content shown 7–10 days later.
- **Handling leads:** set up an auto-reply, track leads in a CRM (or at least a Google Sheet), and keep 5–10 saved replies for common questions (price, scheduling, "need to ask my spouse").
- **Track every day:** spend, leads, cost per lead, sales and revenue.

## 6. Bonus shortcuts
1. **Check the Meta Ad Library.** Competitor ads that have run for months are probably working, so study their hook, format and copy.
2. **Reuse the post ID.** Run the ad to a warm audience first so it collects likes and comments, then use that same post ID in cold campaigns so the social proof carries over.
3. **Duplicate winners right away** with one change (new hook, thumbnail or headline). Winners fade fast.
4. **Use customer testimonials:** 15-second vertical videos shot on a phone, used as Reels or Stories.
5. **Skip expensive tracking tools at the start.** CTR, CPM and comments tell you enough. Polls and DMs with your audience give you their exact words to use in copy.
6. **Test hooks organically first** as posts or captions. If one does well for free, it will likely do well as an ad.
7. **Sell before you build.** Run ads to a pre-order landing page to check demand.

---

**Things to watch out for:**
- **The objective names are outdated.** Ads Manager no longer has a standalone "Messages" or "Conversions" objective. Today you'd choose Engagement, Leads or Sales and then set the conversion location to messaging apps.
- **The targeting advice contradicts itself.** "Start broad" but also "don't use Advantage+ audience" — even though Meta now pushes Advantage+ as its broad option. It's worth testing both instead of taking either rule as fixed.
- **Benchmarks are US local-service numbers.** CPMs and costs per lead in Romania, and for B2B SaaS, will be quite different. For a B2B product, a Leads objective with an instant form or a landing page usually fits better than the Messages default for home services.
